### 6. Everything new that I derived first: agrees
- The five predict blocks: the marked option is right in each, and each why-text gives a true reason for the wrong
  options. The key-ideas box, the four hints, the non-example, the new check "t(ρ) by powers", the seven typical
  mistakes, the five rows of the summary table, the "what to remember" list and the six self-check answers: no
  false statement found (the closing line is finding 2).
- General statements, with proofs:
  - `t(ρ) = ρ u ρ^2 u ... u ρ^n` on n elements: true. A shortest walk from x to z of length k > n would repeat a
    vertex among its last k vertices, and cutting the loop out leaves a shorter walk of length at least 1. The
    bound n is needed (a cycle on n vertices gets its loops only from ρ^n). The page states the condition
    ("when A has n elements").
  - "symmetric and antisymmetric iff ρ ⊆ Δ_A": true. If (x,y) ∈ ρ then (y,x) ∈ ρ by S and x = y by AS; the
    converse is immediate. The empty relation and the diagonal are both included; 2^n such relations.
  - "t(s(r(ρ))) is the smallest equivalence containing ρ": true. r gives R; s keeps R and gives S; t keeps R and S
    and gives T; and every equivalence containing ρ contains all three closures in turn. What matters is that t
    comes after s; r may come anywhere (0 failures for t(s(r)), t(r(s)), r(t(s)) on 2, 3 and 4 elements).
    Counterexample for a wrong order: ρ = {(1,2),(1,3)} on {1,2,3}: s(t(r(ρ))) has (2,1) and (1,3) but not (2,3),
    so it is not transitive (48 of 512 relations on 3 elements fail, 7328 of 65 536 on 4). The page's own example
    {(1,2)} on {1,2} shows s(t(ρ)) ≠ t(s(ρ)), which is what its sentence «Կարգը կարևոր է» claims.
