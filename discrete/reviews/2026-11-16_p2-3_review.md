- **General statements, with proofs:**
  - `t(ρ) = ρ ∪ ρ² ∪ ... ∪ ρ^n` on n elements: true. Take a shortest walk from x to z; if it had more than n
    arrows, two of its vertices after the start would coincide, and cutting out the piece between them leaves a
    shorter walk of at least one arrow. The bound n is needed (a cycle on n vertices gets its loops only from
    ρ^n; ρ ∪ ... ∪ ρ^(n-1) fails for 3 of 16, 38 of 512, 1338 of 65 536 relations). The page states the condition
    («երբ $A$-ն $n$ տարր ունի»).
  - "symmetric and antisymmetric iff ρ ⊆ Δ_A": true. If (x,y) ∈ ρ then (y,x) ∈ ρ by S and x = y by AS; the
    converse is immediate. The empty relation and the diagonal are both included; 2^n such relations (8 on three
    elements, as the page says).
  - "t(s(r(ρ))) is the smallest equivalence containing ρ": true. r gives R; s keeps R and gives S; t keeps R and S
    (powers of a symmetric relation are symmetric) and gives T; and every equivalence containing ρ contains the
    three closures in turn. What matters is t after s; r may come anywhere (0 failures for t(s(r)), t(r(s)),
    r(t(s)) on 2, 3 and 4 elements). **Counterexample for a wrong order:** ρ = {(1,2),(1,3)} on {1,2,3}:
    s(t(r(ρ))) contains (2,1) and (1,3) but not (2,3), so it is not transitive (48 of 512 relations fail on 3
    elements, 7328 of 65 536 on 4). The page's own example {(1,2)} on {1,2} shows s(t(ρ)) ≠ t(s(ρ)), which is what
    its sentence «Կարգը կարևոր է» claims; with r included nothing fails on two elements, so the page's example
    could not have been used for the three-closure statement, and it is not.
  - "the five maps commute on all of Z": 11 of the 25 ordered pairs commute, and they do so on all of Z, not only
    on -1000..1000: five are a map with itself, and the other three pairs are the identities `|x|² = x² = |x²|`,
    `2(-x) = -(2x)`, `|2x| = 2|x|`. Each of the other 14 pairs differs at the x the widget prints.
  - The closing line: finding 4.
- **Edge cases:** the empty relation is S, AS and T but not R (on a non-empty set); the diagonal has all four and is
  both an equivalence and a partial order; the full relation is an equivalence and not AS (n ≥ 2). The editor says
